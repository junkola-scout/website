The Enemy

Communication clutter.

Conversations happen. Then they vanish. Decisions get made in meetings no one wrote down. Commitments get buried in threads. Institutional memory lives in one overworked head.

? What happened this week? What changed? Who owns what? What’s stuck? What’s at risk? What got missed

Ask any of these on a random Tuesday and most companies need a week of Slack archaeology to answer. Multiply that across every team, every week — the cost lands on the people holding it all together in their heads.

The Pain

Where the cost actually shows up.

Three patterns drain the most time and money in growing companies — and they all trace back to conversation that was never captured.

Decisions get re-litigated

Re-litigating past decisions destroys team momentum, wastes expensive leadership hours, and undermines alignment. Solving this by making every decision fully traceable to its original conversation and evidence provides immediate, high-ROI relief.

Problems surface too late

Surprise emergencies are among the most costly operational failures in growing companies. Automatically surfacing repeating minor risks before they balloon into critical incidents saves direct revenue, prevents churn, and avoids expensive fire-drills.

Important commitments get forgotten

Operational execution breaks down when informal commitments made in meetings or chats vanish. Automatically catching promises, assigning explicit RACI ownership, and keeping them visible converts raw conversation directly into tracked accountability.

Our StoryWhy “Junkola”?

We built the thing first. The name came later — much later.

Once we saw what it could actually do — take the mess a company generates every day and turn it into a clear picture of what is really going on — naming it stopped feeling like the hard part.

So somebody made a joke about it: if the product is this good, we could call it Junkola and people would still want it.

Nobody laughed hard enough to talk us out of it. The name stuck, the arguing stopped, and we went back to work.

There is a serious version too. The word is honest about what goes in: half-finished threads, meetings nobody wrote down, a decision buried in a chat from March. Junk in. Clarity out. Most software names promise you the output — ours admits the input.

Turns out that is the whole pitch.
